# l3-backprop-theory — Backpropagation, Derived

Concept: Backprop is the chain rule organized as bookkeeping over a computational graph — forward values, backward gradients, local-gradient × upstream-gradient at every edge, SUM at fan-out — proved by deriving it, then encoded as a scalar autograd engine. Curriculum sequence (HANDOVERFINAL §13): StatQuest video = the mental unlock → manual derivation of a tiny network = the proof → Karpathy micrograd = the competence.
Learner prerequisites: l2-multivariable at Gold (chain rule, partials — hard gate), l3-sgd-optimizers (knows what the gradients are FOR). Python fluency from L1. Karpathy's own stated bar for the micrograd lecture — "basic knowledge of Python and a vague recollection of calculus from high school" (fetched from the nn-zero-to-hero README this session) — is exactly this learner's profile.
What beginners commonly misunderstand:
- Backprop as a mystical/biological or approximate procedure, when it is exact chain-rule bookkeeping (the node's own listed misconception).
- The multivariable chain rule's SUM over paths: why a node feeding two consumers adds its upstream gradients (weight sharing, residual wiring later — this single point predicts most future confusion).
- Local vs global: trying to hold the entire end-to-end derivative in one head-sized expression instead of trusting local gradient × upstream gradient composed edge-by-edge.
- Symbol shapes: ∂L/∂W must have W's shape; the Xᵀδ vs δXᵀ transposition panic when vectorizing.
- Believing that watching a derivation confers the ability to produce one (the recognition trap this node's Gold gate exists to kill).
